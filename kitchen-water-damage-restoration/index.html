Why kitchens are hard to dry.

Base cabinets sit on the subfloor and run wall to wall. Water that gets under them has nowhere to go, and the cabinet boxes shield it from airflow. Dishwashers, refrigerators and sink bases add pipes and hoses that can leak into the same closed space.

The result is a room that looks nearly dry on top while the floor and cabinet bases stay wet underneath.

The usual sources of a kitchen leak.

  • A split or loose supply line under the sink.
  • A dishwasher door gasket, inlet valve or drain hose.
  • A refrigerator ice maker line.
  • A clogged or failed drain that backs up into the sink base.
  • A leak from an upstairs bathroom that comes through the ceiling.

Most of these start as drips. Check under the sink and behind the dishwasher for damp, swollen or discolored cabinet material.

Protecting the claim record.

The New York Department of Financial Services recommends photographing damage before cleanup and making only temporary repairs until the insurer inspects. In a kitchen, keep the failed hose or part, list the items you discard and keep receipts for any emergency purchases.

We provide readings and photos of the work we perform so your adjuster can see the starting condition and the finish.

Drying a kitchen without tearing it apart.

When cabinets can stay, small holes in toe-kicks let warm air flow behind them. We connect that to our structural drying setup so the floor and wall bases dry together. Sometimes a few cabinet bases must come out. We tell you which before we touch them.
