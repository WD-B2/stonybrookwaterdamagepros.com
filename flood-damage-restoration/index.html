Why flood damage needs a different approach.

Floodwater picks up dirt, debris, fertilizer, fuel and sometimes sewage on its way into a building. That is why flood cleanup is not just a drying job. Porous materials that absorbed contaminated water, such as carpet, pad, insulation and some drywall, often have to be removed instead of cleaned.

The industry standard for water damage, ANSI/IICRC S500, groups water by contamination level. We use that way of thinking to decide what can be dried and what cannot: clean water from a supply line is handled differently from gray water, and both differ from black water that carries sewage or floodwater from the ground.

Flood emergency: what to do when floodwater is in the building.

Safety before cleanup.

  • Stay out of standing water if the power is on or if outlets are submerged..
  • Avoid skin contact with floodwater and wash with soap and clean water if you touch it..
  • Do not run a household vacuum or use fans on wet outlets..
  • Photograph the water level, damage and belongings before moving anything..
  • Write down what you discard. Your insurer may ask for an inventory..
  • If floodwater reaches outlets, the panel or a gas appliance, leave and call 911 or your utility before calling us. Never enter water that may be energized..

Flood, insurance and what New York advises.

The New York Department of Financial Services states that flood damage is generally covered only by flood insurance, usually through the National Flood Insurance Program administered by FEMA. It recommends filing claims promptly, photographing damage before cleanup and making only temporary repairs until the insurer inspects. Keep an inventory and a log of what you do.

We keep moisture readings and photographs of our work so you have a clear record for any claim conversation.
