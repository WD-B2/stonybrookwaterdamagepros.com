What changes from one part of the area to another.

Water problems on the North Shore are not all alike. Properties near the harbor, ponds and wetlands deal with high water tables, tidal influence and storm runoff. Properties farther inland are more often hit by plumbing failures, roof leaks and appliance losses. A basement that stays dry for years can flood in one unusual storm, as the August 2024 rain showed when almost 10 inches fell in parts of Suffolk County.

Septic systems and cesspools add another variable. Suffolk County states in its Septic Improvement Program FAQ that 74% of Suffolk County residents rely on antiquated cesspools and septic systems. When one of those systems backs up, the water is contaminated and the cleanup follows different rules. See sewage backup cleanup.

Older buildings and newer ones also behave differently. Plaster, wood floors and original trim dry differently from modern drywall and engineered flooring. We check what a building is made of before choosing how to dry it, and we explain the plan first.

Getting the right crew to the right address.

Dispatch looks at the type of loss, the size of the affected area and the time of the call. A burst pipe in a small apartment needs a different crew and equipment than a flooded retail space. Telling the dispatcher these details early helps us send what the job needs the first time.

If the water is contaminated, say so. Sewage and floodwater call for protective equipment and a different plan, and we want the right crew on the way from the start.

Why we keep the area list short.

We would rather serve a defined area well than promise coverage we cannot support. The towns listed here are the ones our crews reach regularly from Stony Brook. If your address is just outside the list, call anyway, because the dispatcher can tell you honestly whether we can reach you and how soon.

When a loss is in a town we do not serve, we say so right away so you can call someone else without losing time. Water damage does not wait, and neither should you.

What to do before you call.

  • Your address and the nearest cross street or landmark..
  • Where the water is and whether it is still running..
  • Whether you have closed the main valve or turned off an appliance..
  • What the water touched, such as carpet, hardwood, drywall or ceilings..
  • Whether the water looks or smells contaminated..

Local rules and resources.

For permits and building questions, contact the Town of Brookhaven Division of Building, which sits under Planning and Environment. Town Hall is at 1 Independence Hill in Farmingville, and the main number is 631-451-TOWN. Incorporated villages such as Port Jefferson may have their own offices, so confirm which one applies to your address.

To check flood risk, use the FEMA flood map. For mold rules, see the New York State Department of Labor mold program, which we summarize on our mold remediation page.
