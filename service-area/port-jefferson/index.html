What water damage looks like along Port Jefferson Harbor.

The village has two centers about a mile apart on Main Street, which is NY 25A. Lower Port Jefferson sits on the waterfront near the Bridgeport and Port Jefferson Ferry and Harborfront Park, completed in 2004. Upper Port Jefferson climbs the hill to the south. Setauket Harbor adjoins the village, and the Village Historic District has been on the National Register since 2005.

The waterfront has a long history with water. The harborfront flooded at high tide until a causeway was built in 1836. Today shops, restaurants and homes near the harbor face the same basic problem on a smaller scale: low ground, tides and storm runoff. A cellar or ground-floor unit near the water can take on water quickly in a heavy rain.

Older village buildings also bring older finishes. Plaster, wood floors and original trim each dry differently, which is why we map moisture before cutting or pulling anything. See our pages on wall damage and hardwood floors for how we handle them.
