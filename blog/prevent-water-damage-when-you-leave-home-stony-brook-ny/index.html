How to Prevent Water Damage When You Leave Home.

A leaving-home checklist for shutoffs, heat, hoses and sump pumps, plus what to do if you return to water.

24/7 dispatch line.

(934) 223-1875.

Call now.

By the Stony Brook Water Damage Pros team. Updated 2026-11-12.

The short answer.

Before you leave, shut off the main water valve for long trips, keep the heat on in winter, check hoses and the sump pump, and ask someone to look in on the house. Most big water losses while owners are away come from a failed hose, a frozen pipe or a pump that stops. A short checklist before you travel removes most of that risk.

This guide gives a practical leaving-home routine and explains what to do if you return and find water.

Before you go: a quick checklist.

  • Close the main shutoff valve if you will be gone for several days..
  • Turn off the water heater or lower its setting if the water is off..
  • Inspect washing machine and dishwasher hoses and close their supply valves..
  • Check under sinks for drips..
  • Clear gutters and downspouts so rain moves away from the foundation..
  • Test the sump pump by pouring water into the pit..

Winter: keep the pipes from freezing.

Frozen pipes crack during the thaw. If you will be away in cold weather, keep the heat on at a moderate setting rather than turning it off. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm air reaches the pipes. Insulate pipes in basements, crawl spaces and garages.

See our page on frozen pipe damage for what happens when a pipe fails, and burst pipe cleanup for the next steps.

Summer and storm season.

  • Check the roof, flashing and gutters before storm season.
  • Look at the grading around the foundation and make sure water flows away.
  • Know where your shutoff valves are and test them.
  • Consider a backup power source or battery backup for a sump pump.

Who should check the house.

Ask a neighbor, friend or house sitter to visit every few days. Give them the main shutoff location and your phone number. A quick walk-through, including the basement, catches a leak early. If you are gone for a season, consider a regular property check.

What if you come home to water.

Do this first.

  • Do not walk into standing water if outlets or cords may be wet..
  • Close the main valve if you can reach it safely..
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything..
  • Call the 24/7 line for removal and drying..

Tell the crew how long the house was empty, because that changes the drying plan. See water extraction and basement water damage for what happens next.

Insurance and leaving the house.

Some policies expect you to keep the heat on or shut off the water if a home will be vacant for a period. Read your policy or ask your agent about vacancy and winter clauses. The New York State Department of Financial Services also advises documenting damage and making only temporary repairs until the insurer inspects.

Seasonal homes and long absences.

If the house is empty for weeks or months, the risk grows. A property manager or a qualified neighbor can run water, check systems and watch for problems. Shutting off the main and draining lines is an option for some homes, but ask a plumber about the right approach for yours.

A printable-style routine for the day you leave.

  • Walk through every room and look for drips, stains and damp spots..
  • Run the dishwasher and washer one last time, then close their supply valves..
  • Set the thermostat and note it on your list..
  • Close the main valve if you will be gone several days..
  • Photograph the house for your records..
  • Leave contact details with your checker..

Doing this in the same order each time makes it easy to remember. Put the list inside a cabinet door so it is there when you need it.

Rental properties and seasonal tenants.

If you rent out a home or a room, give tenants the shutoff locations and the number to call. Ask them to report drips and stains promptly. Between tenants, walk the property and test fixtures. Many large losses in rentals start with small leaks that went unreported for weeks.

If you manage several properties, a simple annual inspection of hoses, water heaters and sump pumps reduces risk across all of them.

Check the sump pump and the outside of the house.

A sump pump that works in a dry month may fail in a storm. Lift the float and listen for the motor, then check that the discharge pipe is clear and points away from the foundation. If you have a battery backup, check its charge.

Outside, look at the downspouts, window wells and the ground around the foundation. Soil should slope away from the house, and downspouts should carry water several feet from the walls. A few minutes with a hose can show you where water goes.

Sources and standards.

This guide draws on the references below. Check them directly for the current rules that apply to you.

  • ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration, cited as an industry reference and not as a claim of certification.
  • New York State Department of Financial Services consumer guidance on filing and documenting water damage claims.

Leaving a Stony Brook home for the season.

Many Stony Brook residents travel in summer or winter, and the area has both coastal storms and cold snaps. The August 2024 storm, with almost 10 inches of rain in parts of Suffolk County, arrived while many people were away for the end of summer.

Stony Brook University canceled move-in during that storm and about 150 students were displaced, a reminder that even institutional buildings are exposed. If you own a rental or a second home here, plan checks around forecasts. Our service area page shows the towns we serve, including Port Jefferson.
