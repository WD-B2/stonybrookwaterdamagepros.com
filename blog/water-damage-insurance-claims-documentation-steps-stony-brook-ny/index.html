Water Damage Insurance Claim Documentation: Steps That Help.

A step-by-step list of photos, logs and records that make a water damage claim easier to follow.

24/7 dispatch line.

(934) 223-1875.

Call now.

By the Stony Brook Water Damage Pros team. Updated 2026-11-12.

The short answer.

Document the damage before you clean up, record what you do afterward and keep everything in one place. The strongest water damage claim file has dated photos and video of the damage, an inventory of damaged items, receipts for emergency costs and a log of conversations with the insurer. Moisture readings and drying logs from a restoration crew add useful detail about the work.

The New York State Department of Financial Services recommends filing claims promptly, photographing damage before cleanup, making only temporary repairs until the insurer inspects, and keeping an inventory and a log. The steps below follow that advice.

Step 1: Photograph and film before you move anything.

Take wide shots of each affected room, then close-ups of the water line, stains, wet materials and damaged belongings. Include the source if you can see it, such as a split hose or a leaking fixture. Take video as you walk through, narrating what you see and the date.

Photograph the property from outside as well. For storm losses, photograph the roof, windows or grading where water got in, if it is safe to do so.

Step 2: Stop the damage and keep a record of how.

Policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. That means closing a shutoff valve, extracting standing water and protecting belongings. Write down what you did, when and who did it. Keep receipts.

Step 3: Build an inventory.

A simple inventory format.
Item.Room.Age or purchase date.Condition after water.Photo taken.
Sofa.Living room.Approx. 4 years.Soaked, will not dry.Yes.
Laptop.Office.Approx. 2 years.Wet, unplugged.Yes.
Carpet and pad.Basement.Installed at purchase.Soaked.Yes.

Do not throw away damaged items until the insurer has had a chance to see them, unless they are a health risk. If you must discard something, photograph it first and note what it was.

Step 4: Keep a log.

  • Date and time you discovered the loss.
  • Who you called and what was said.
  • Claim number and adjuster name.
  • Dates of inspections and repairs.
  • Emergency costs, such as hotel stays and equipment.

Step 5: Ask your restoration company what it records.

A restoration crew can provide moisture readings, a description of the areas affected, photos of the work and a log of drying equipment and days. These records show how the building was dried and when it reached normal levels. They are most useful when they are dated and clear.

Our structural drying page explains what daily readings look like. Our water extraction page covers what happens before drying begins.

Step 6: Keep copies and share them carefully.

Store your photos and documents in two places, such as your phone and a cloud folder. Send the insurer copies and keep the originals. Keep a list of what you sent and when.

Common mistakes that weaken a claim.

  • Cleaning up before taking photos..
  • Throwing away damaged belongings with no record..
  • Making permanent repairs before the insurer inspects..
  • Waiting weeks to report the loss..
  • Not asking what the policy covers before assuming it does or does not..

Insurance and flood.

If water came from outside the building, remember that flood damage is generally covered only by flood insurance, according to the Department of Financial Services. Ask your agent which policy applies before you assume.

If you have a loss right now, start with the first three steps and call the 24/7 line. We can handle burst pipe losses and flood cleanup with records that are ready for your adjuster.

What a good photo set looks like in practice.

Start at the doorway of each room and shoot toward the farthest corner, then turn and shoot the other way. Take two or three overlapping photos of each wall, the floor and the ceiling. Add close-ups of anything that tells a story, such as a water line on a wall, a swollen baseboard, a stained ceiling tile or a failed fitting. A tape measure or a coin next to damage gives a sense of scale.

Keep the original files rather than screenshots or messaging app copies, since originals hold the date and time. If your phone offers location tagging, leave it on for the loss photos. Back up the set the same day.

Handling the adjuster visit.

Before the adjuster arrives, gather your photos, inventory, receipts and the restoration records. Walk the adjuster through the areas in the order the water traveled. Show moisture readings if you have them and point out hidden areas that were opened. Ask what the adjuster needs from you and when, and write down the answers.

After the visit, send a short follow-up message that lists what was discussed and what you agreed to send. A written trail is the simplest protection you can have if details are questioned later.
