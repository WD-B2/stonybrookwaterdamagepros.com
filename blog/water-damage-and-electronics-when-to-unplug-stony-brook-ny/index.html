Water Damage and Electronics: What to Do First.

What to do with wet electronics, from unplugging safely to deciding when a device needs a technician.

24/7 dispatch line.

(934) 223-1875.

Call now.

By the Stony Brook Water Damage Pros team. Updated 2026-11-12.

The short answer.

If water has reached outlets, cords or devices, make sure it is safe before you touch anything, then unplug devices only if you can do so without standing in water or touching wet cords. Do not power on a device that got wet, even if it looks dry. Drying and checking by a technician is safer than testing it yourself.

This guide covers the electrical safety basics, how to handle laptops, phones, televisions and appliances after water exposure and when to call an electrician.

Electrical safety first.

Before you touch anything.

  • Do not stand in water if outlets, power strips or cords are wet..
  • If you can reach the electrical panel without standing in water, turn off power to the affected area..
  • If the panel is in a wet area, do not go near it. Call an electrician or the utility..
  • Do not use electrical devices to remove water..
  • Photograph the situation before moving anything..

Water and electricity do not mix, and the safest approach is to take the power away from wet areas first. A shock hazard is more serious than any device loss.

What to do with common devices.

Why you should not turn on a wet device.

Moisture inside a device can cause short circuits when power is applied. Even if the device looks dry, residue and trapped water can cause damage or a safety hazard. Waiting for a technician check is the better choice, especially for devices with batteries.

Contaminated water changes things.

Floodwater and sewage leave residue that can corrode components. Devices that touched contaminated water often cannot be safely cleaned. Photograph them, list them and ask a technician before deciding. See flood damage restoration for how we handle contaminated water in buildings.

Wiring and outlets.

Wet outlets, wiring and panels should be checked by a licensed electrician before power is restored. Drywall and insulation that soaked water around wiring may need removal as well. Our crews note electrical hazards and tell you when an electrician is needed. See wall and drywall water damage repair.

Document for insurance.

  • Photograph each device and the water around it..
  • List the make, model and age..
  • Keep receipts for replacements..
  • Ask your insurer what proof it needs..

The New York State Department of Financial Services recommends keeping an inventory and making only temporary repairs until the insurer inspects.

Data recovery.

If you have important data on a wet device, power it off and contact a data recovery service before trying to turn it on. Back up devices regularly so you are not at risk.

Preventing water and electronics problems.

  • Keep electronics off basement floors.
  • Use surge protectors above floor level.
  • Know where your electrical panel is and keep access clear.
  • Use leak sensors near water heaters and appliances.

When an electrician needs to check the building.

After a flood or a major leak, ask an electrician to inspect any panel, outlet, switch or fixture that was exposed to water. Even if everything looks dry, moisture inside walls can create a hazard when power returns. Do not rely on a quick test with a plug-in device.

The electrician may recommend replacing outlets, breakers or wiring that were submerged. Keep invoices and photographs for insurance.

Large appliances and home systems.

  • Refrigerators and freezers: unplug, empty and have a technician check them.
  • Furnaces and water heaters: do not relight or restart after water exposure. Call a licensed technician.
  • Washers and dryers: check the motor and controls before use.
  • Sump pumps and well pumps: have them inspected after a flood.

Backing up before the next loss.

The best protection for your data is a backup that lives somewhere else. Use cloud backup or an external drive kept away from the main living space, and check it from time to time to make sure it works. Photos, tax records and work files are the usual priorities.

For devices you cannot back up, such as a home server or a gaming console, keep them off the floor and away from exterior walls and windows. A small shelf can be the difference between a close call and a replacement.

Handling batteries and chargers.

  • Do not charge a wet phone or laptop. Charging can cause a short circuit.
  • Remove removable batteries if you can do so safely.
  • Swollen or hot batteries should be kept away from flammable items and handled by a technician.
  • Replace chargers and cables that were exposed to water.

Sources and standards.

This guide draws on the references below. Check them directly for the current rules that apply to you.

Electronics and storms in Stony Brook.

Stony Brook University, with its main campus under two miles from the historic center, canceled move-in during the August 2024 storm and about 150 students were displaced. Laptops and devices are among the first things people worry about in an event like that.

If you live in a basement apartment or a flood-prone area, store electronics above floor level and keep backups. Ask the Town of Brookhaven Division of Building about electrical permits for repair work. See our service area page and Port Jefferson page for towns we serve.
