What to Keep or Throw Away After Water Damage.

How water type, material and time decide which belongings can be saved and which should go.

24/7 dispatch line.

(934) 223-1875.

Call now.

By the Stony Brook Water Damage Pros team. Updated 2026-11-12.

The short answer.

Whether to keep or discard a belonging after water damage depends on three things: the kind of water, how long the item stayed wet and what the item is made of. Hard, non-porous items touched by clean water are usually safe to clean and keep. Porous items such as mattresses, upholstered furniture and carpet pad that soaked contaminated water are usually discarded.

This guide gives a practical way to sort your belongings and explains what to photograph and list before anything leaves the house.

Step one: figure out the water type.

Clean water from a supply line behaves very differently from gray water or black water. Our guide on water damage categories covers the details, and the rule of thumb is simple: if the water came from sewage, a drain or the ground outside, treat it as contaminated.

Step two: sort by material.

Step three: photograph and list before you discard.

  • Take a photo of each item and where it was..
  • Write the item, its age and approximate value..
  • Keep a sample of carpet or fabric if an adjuster may want it..
  • Keep damaged items you can safely store until the insurer inspects..

If an item is a health hazard, photograph it, note what it was and then discard it. The New York State Department of Financial Services recommends keeping an inventory and a log.

Items that deserve extra care.

  • Photographs and papers. Freeze or air-dry them quickly, and see a guide on saving them.
  • Electronics. Unplug and do not power them on until a technician checks them.
  • Mattresses. They soak water and are hard to sanitize.
  • Food in the fridge and pantry that touched floodwater.
  • Medicines and cosmetics exposed to floodwater.

Items that often clean up well.

Dishes, glassware, metal tools, plastic toys and hard-surface furniture can often be washed and disinfected when they were exposed to clean or gray water. For floodwater, use soap, water and a disinfectant suited to the surface, wear gloves and let items dry fully.

Building materials follow the same logic.

Hard surfaces such as tile and concrete clean up well. Porous materials such as carpet pad, insulation and drywall soak water and are often removed when contaminated. See carpet water damage, wall and drywall repair and flood damage restoration for details.

When to let a professional decide.

If you have valuable or sentimental items, ask a restoration company about contents cleaning and pack-out services. Some items can be cleaned in a controlled setting. Others are not worth the risk.

Do not rush the throwing away.

It is tempting to clear everything out right away. Pause to document. Once a damaged item is gone, you cannot show an insurer what it was. Take photographs first and keep a list.

How to clean items that can be kept.

  2. 2

    Wash.

    Use soap and clean water to remove dirt and residue.

Do not mix cleaning chemicals. Some combinations create harmful fumes. Work in a ventilated space and keep children and pets away.

Ask before you discard large items.

Large items such as couches, mattresses and appliances can be expensive to replace, so ask your insurer before discarding them. Photograph them, note the model and size and ask whether an adjuster wants to see them. If you do not get an answer quickly, document and store the item safely until you do.

A simple three-box method.

Set up three areas: keep, clean and discard. Items that are clearly fine go in the keep area. Items that need washing or disinfecting go in the clean area. Items that are contaminated or ruined go in the discard area, but only after you have photographed and listed them.

Working in this order keeps the process organized and gives you a clear record. It also stops you from spending hours cleaning an item that was never going to be safe to keep.

Take care of yourself while sorting.

  • Wear gloves, eye protection and sturdy shoes.
  • Take breaks and drink water, because sorting soaked belongings is tiring.
  • Do not lift heavy wet items alone.
  • Ask a friend or family member to help with the emotional side of deciding what to let go.

Sources and standards.

This guide draws on the references below. Check them directly for the current rules that apply to you.

Sorting belongings after a Stony Brook loss.

Many Stony Brook households keep storage in basements and garages, which are the areas hit hardest by heavy rain and backups. The August 2024 storm damaged six houses, including cesspools, and Stony Brook University canceled move-in as about 150 students were displaced.
