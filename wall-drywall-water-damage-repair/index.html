How water gets into walls.

Water reaches walls from above through ceilings, from the side through leaking pipes and from below as it wicks up from a wet floor. Gypsum board and insulation hold water, and the paper face is food for mold.

A wall can look fine while the cavity behind it is wet. Meters and a thermal camera show what paint conceals.

Signs of wall water damage.

Stains and discoloration.

Yellow or brown marks that spread over time.

Bubbling or peeling paint.

Moisture pushes the finish away from the board.

Soft or crumbly drywall.

Pressing the wall gives or the surface flakes.

Warping or swelling.

Baseboards and seams lift or pull away.

Why wall damage is often bigger than it looks.

Water wicks upward in drywall, sometimes well above the visible stain. A flood cut, a clean horizontal cut a few inches above the highest moisture reading, removes the wet section and leaves a straight edge for the repair.

If the cause was above, see ceiling water damage repair. If the cause was a floor problem, see hardwood floor repair and carpet restoration.

Plaster, paneling and older wall types.

Not every wall is drywall. Plaster over lath, wood paneling and tile backer boards respond differently to water. Plaster can hold moisture for a long time and sometimes dries well with injected air. Paneling often warps and may need to come off to dry the framing behind it.

We check what the wall is made of before we choose a method. Cutting into plaster the wrong way creates dust and a bigger repair than the water ever did, so we plan the opening carefully and explain it first.

If your building has older finishes, tell the dispatcher when you call. It helps us bring the right tools and set the right expectations for the repair.

Moisture in a wall also reaches the floor and the ceiling. If the stain follows a plumbing path, check the rooms above and below as well. A single leak often damages two floors.
