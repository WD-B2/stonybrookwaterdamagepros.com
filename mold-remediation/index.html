Signs you may have a mold problem.

Visible spots.

Dark, green or white patches on walls, ceilings, baseboards or around windows.

Persistent musty odor.

A damp smell that returns after cleaning.

Past water damage.

A leak, flood or wet carpet that was not dried quickly.

Peeling or bubbling paint.

Moisture behind the surface.

Seeing mold does not tell you how much is behind it. A trained assessor can evaluate the extent. We deal with the moisture and the remediation work.

How New York handles larger mold jobs.

The New York State Department of Labor runs a mold program. For mold areas over 10 square feet, licensed professionals are required. The mold assessor and the mold remediator must be separate. A written remediation plan comes first, and a post-remediation clearance check follows the work. Property owners may work on their own property.

You can look up licensed contractors on the Department of Labor tool. We explain how the process works and coordinate with the licensed professionals involved. We do not perform assessments and we do not make health claims about mold.

What we do and do not do on a mold call.

We look at the moisture history first: when the water event happened, what was wet and how long it stayed that way. That tells us whether the problem is likely a past, dried-out event or an active leak that is still feeding growth. Moisture readings and a look at hidden areas, such as the back of a wall base or the underside of a stained ceiling, fill in the rest.

We do not write the remediation plan or perform the post-remediation clearance check, because New York requires separate licensed professionals for that work on larger areas. We do explain what the plan will probably cover, schedule our drying and repair work around the licensed team and keep the building owner informed throughout.

We also do not claim that any mold is harmless or harmful. If someone in the household has health concerns, that is a conversation for a physician.

A note for Stony Brook buildings near the water.

Stony Brook sits beside the harbor and Mill Pond, and the August 2024 storm showed how quickly a wet season can saturate properties nearby. Basements and crawl spaces in damp settings dry slowly, so mold risk rises when a loss is not dried promptly. Ask the Town of Brookhaven building department about permits for any repair work that opens walls or changes the structure.

Prevent mold by drying fast.

  • Dry wet materials within a couple of days.
  • Fix leaks right away.
  • Run exhaust fans and keep humidity in check.
  • Replace wet carpet pad and insulation that cannot be dried.

Fast action after a loss matters more than any product. Our water extraction and drying services aim to remove the conditions mold needs.
