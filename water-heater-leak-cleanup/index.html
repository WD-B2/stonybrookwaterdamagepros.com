When a water heater leak becomes an emergency.

A tank water heater holds a large volume of water under constant use. When corrosion or sediment wears through the tank, the leak can start as a slow drip and turn into a flood when the bottom lets go. Many homeowners find the problem only when the floor is already wet.

Water heaters often sit in basements, closets or utility rooms with finished walls or stored belongings nearby. That means a small failure can reach drywall, boxes, carpet and the floor system quickly.

Safe steps while you wait for help.

What to do before help arrives.

  • Turn off the power to the heater at the breaker if you can do it safely. For a gas unit, turn the gas control to the off position..
  • Close the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the tank..
  • Do not step into standing water near the heater if any outlet or cord is wet..
  • Photograph the unit, the puddle and any damaged belongings before moving them..
  • If you smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility before calling us. Do not relight a pilot that was exposed to water..

Do not drain a hot tank without care. The water inside can be hot enough to burn. If you are unsure, leave the unit alone until the plumber arrives.

What we clean up after a water heater failure.

Standing water.

We remove it with pumps and extractors so it does not wick into walls or flooring.

Lower walls and trim.

Drywall and baseboards near the unit are checked and cut back if they stay wet.

Flooring.

Carpet, pad and floating floors are evaluated for drying or removal.

Drying.

Air movers and dehumidifiers bring the structure back to normal moisture levels.

Repair or replacement is a plumber question.

We handle the water damage. A licensed plumber decides whether to repair or replace the unit. We time our work so wet materials can be removed and drying can begin promptly. If the leak is not fixed, drying cannot hold.

Signs your heater may be close to failing.

  • Rust-colored water from the hot tap.
  • Puddles or damp spots around the base of the tank.
  • Rumbling or popping from sediment inside the tank.
  • Moisture on the pipes and fittings above the unit.

If you notice any of these, a plumber can inspect the unit before it becomes a flood.

Water heater leaks and insurance.

Sudden tank failure may be treated differently from long-term seepage. The New York Department of Financial Services recommends photographing damage before cleanup and making only temporary repairs until the insurer inspects. We keep records of readings and work performed so you have a clear file.
