Written by the Stony Brook Water Damage Pros team. Updated 2026-11-12.

Water damage comes in many forms, and the right response depends on the source, the water type and the materials affected. The groups below follow the way losses usually arrive. Most jobs combine two or three of these services, for example extraction followed by structural drying and then a wall or floor repair.

Pipes and plumbing.

Burst Pipe Water Damage.

Cleanup after a supply line splits and floods floors, walls or ceilings.

Frozen Pipe Water Damage.

Water removal and drying when a frozen pipe cracks during the thaw.

Water Heater Leak Cleanup.

Extraction and drying after a tank leaks or fails.

Slab Leak Water Damage.

Moisture mapping and drying when a line under the slab leaks.

Appliances and HVAC.

Appliance Leak Water Damage.

Cleanup from washers, dishwashers, refrigerators and ice makers.

AC Leak Water Damage.

Drying and repair after a condensate line or pan overflows.

Kitchen Water Damage.

Cabinet, floor and subfloor drying after a kitchen leak.

Weather and building envelope.

Ceiling Water Damage Repair.

Safe release, drying and repair of ceilings, including roof leaks.

Storm Water Damage.

Wind-driven rain, window and roof leaks and flood intrusion.

Flood Damage Cleanup.

Removal, cleanup and drying after floodwater enters a building.

Basement Water Damage.

Pumping, drying and cleanup for finished and unfinished basements.

Crawl Space Water Damage.

Standing water, wet insulation and damp framing under the house.

Water removal, drying and mold.

Water Extraction.

Pumps and extractors that remove standing water fast.

Structural Drying.

Air movers, dehumidifiers and moisture readings until the structure is dry.

Sewage Backup Cleanup.

Protected removal and cleaning after a sewage or septic backup.

Mold Remediation.

How New York mold rules work and where our drying and repair work fits.

Floors and walls.

Hardwood Floor Water Damage.

Drying and repair plans for cupped or buckled wood floors.

Carpet Water Damage.

Extraction, pad removal and drying for wet carpet.

Wall and Drywall Water Damage.

Drying or removing wet drywall and insulation, then repair.

Commercial.

Commercial Water Damage.

Offices, shops and restaurants that cannot afford downtime.

What to do before you call.

  • Stop the source if you can do it safely..
  • Stay out of rooms where water meets outlets or cords..
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything..
  • Note where the water is coming from and what it touched..

Sources and standards.

Our service pages draw on the references below.

  • ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration.
  • ANSI/IICRC S520 and the New York State Department of Labor mold program, for mold.
  • New York State Department of Financial Services guidance on water damage claims.
