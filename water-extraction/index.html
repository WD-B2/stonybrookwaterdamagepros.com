When standing water is an emergency, the first hours decide the repair.

Water does not stay where it lands. It wicks up drywall, runs under baseboards and slips into the gap between a finished floor and the subfloor below it. Every hour it sits, more material takes it on, and materials that were dry and salvageable become materials that have to be cut out.

Emergency water extraction is the step that stops that spread. A crew removes the bulk water first, then the drying equipment deals with what is left in the structure. Skipping or delaying extraction is the most common reason a small loss turns into a full rebuild.

What counts as an extraction job.

Pooled water on a hard floor, a saturated carpet and pad, an inch of water in a finished basement, or a flooded kitchen after an appliance failure all qualify. If you can see water on the floor or hear it dripping inside a wall cavity, call before you start mopping. We also handle larger flood events and basement flooding with the same equipment.

What to do while you wait for the crew.

Safe steps before we arrive.

  • Stop the source if you can do it safely, such as closing a shutoff valve or turning off a running appliance..
  • Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Stay out of rooms where outlets or cords are submerged..
  • Photograph the damage and the standing water before anything is moved. Your insurer will want to see the original condition..
  • Lift small valuables, paper and electronics out of reach of the water. Leave heavy furniture where it is..
  • If you smell gas, see sparks or hear buzzing from wet outlets, leave the building and call 911 or your utility before calling us..

Do not run a household vacuum on wet floors and do not open walls yourself. Cutting into a wet wall before the water is mapped can spread the problem and make the claim harder to document.

What changes the price of an extraction job.

Cost factors we explain before work starts.

We do not quote a number from a phone call because the same puddle can mean very different work. These are the factors that move the total:

  • Depth and areaInches of water across a whole floor take more time and equipment than a wet corner..
  • Water categoryClean supply water is handled differently from sewage or storm intrusion..
  • Materials affectedCarpet, pad, hardwood and drywall each respond differently once wet..
  • AccessBasements, crawl spaces and upper floors change how hoses and pumps are routed..
  • Follow-up dryingMost jobs continue into drying and monitoring after extraction ends..

Stony Brook conditions that shape an extraction call.

Stony Brook sits on the north shore of Long Island with the harbor, Mill Pond and the West Meadow wetlands close to the village. On August 18 and 19, 2024, almost 10 inches of rain fell in parts of Suffolk County. Mill Pond overflowed that week and part of Harbor Road collapsed, which shows how fast a heavy storm can turn into a water problem for nearby properties.

Many homes in the area rely on cesspools or septic systems rather than public sewers. When floodwater reaches a basement drain or a failing system backs up, the water may not be clean. We treat that as a different job and say so before we begin. See our page on sewage backup cleanup if the water smells or looks cloudy.

If your water loss came from a storm, the storm damage page covers the roof, window and grading side of the problem as well.
