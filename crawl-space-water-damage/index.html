Why a crawl space problem becomes a house problem.

A damp crawl space sits directly under your floors. Moisture rises into subfloors, joists and insulation, and it can reach flooring above. It also holds odors that move into living areas.

Most homeowners learn about it from a musty smell, cold floors, sagging insulation or a pipe leak under the house.

Common causes.

  • Plumbing leaks and failed supply lines. See burst pipe cleanup.
  • Rain or groundwater entering through vents, foundation cracks or poor grading.
  • Condensation on cold pipes and ductwork.
  • Frozen and thawed pipes in winter. See frozen pipe damage.
  • Missing or damaged vapor barriers over bare soil.

What to do when you find water under the house.

Careful steps.

  • Do not crawl into standing water near wires, outlets or equipment..
  • Close the main water valve if a pipe is leaking..
  • Photograph what you can see from the access hatch..
  • Avoid disturbing insulation or soil if it may be contaminated..

Why mold shows up in crawl spaces.

Wood framing and a damp, dark, still space provide what mold needs. If we see growth, we note it and explain your next step. Under New York rules, larger mold areas involve licensed professionals, and the assessor and remediator are separate. Our mold remediation page explains this.

Keeping a crawl space drier.

  • Keep gutters and downspouts clear and pointed away from the foundation.
  • Make sure soil slopes away from the house.
  • Repair plumbing leaks right away.
  • Consider a vapor barrier over exposed ground.
  • Ask a specialist about encapsulation or drainage if water is a recurring problem.

Encapsulation and drainage work is specialist construction. We handle the damage and drying and we describe what we saw.

What we look at when we open the access hatch.

Before we price anything we look at the whole space, not just the puddle. We check the soil or slab for standing or seeping water, the underside of the subfloor for dark staining, the insulation for sag or discoloration and the pipes and ductwork for drips or condensation. We note the vent openings and whether a vapor barrier is present.

Each observation changes the plan. A single leaking fitting needs a different response than a space that takes on groundwater after every heavy rain. We tell you which one you have, with photos, so the decision about repairs is yours.

If the space is too tight, too deep in water or has unsafe wiring, we say so and explain what has to happen before a crew can work. Safety comes before speed in confined areas.
