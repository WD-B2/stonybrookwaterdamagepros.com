How an air conditioner ends up leaking inside your house.

Cooling coils pull moisture out of the air. That water is meant to leave through a drain line. If the line clogs, the drain pan overflows and water runs onto whatever is under or around the unit.

Air handlers often sit in attics, closets and basements, which are places people do not check. A leak can stay hidden until a ceiling stains or a wall feels damp.

Signs of an AC leak.

Ceiling stain.

A brown ring under an attic or second-floor air handler.

Wet closet floor.

Water around the base of a utility closet unit.

Musty smell.

A damp smell near vents or the air handler.

Rust on the unit.

Staining around the pan or the cabinet.

What to do first.

Do these before cleanup begins.

  • Turn off the air conditioner at the thermostat and the breaker so it stops producing condensate..
  • Place a bucket or towels under any active drip..
  • Do not stand under a bulging ceiling, because it can drop..
  • Photograph the unit, the pan and the damage..

A heating and cooling technician can clear the drain line and repair the pan. We handle the wet materials.

Why slow AC leaks matter.

Condensate leaks are usually clean water, but they run for a long time. Constant dampness in insulation and drywall is a setup for mold. If you find staining that keeps coming back, our mold remediation page explains what we look for.

Reducing the chance of a repeat.

  • Have the condensate line flushed as part of regular air conditioner service.
  • Check the drain pan for rust and standing water.
  • Consider a float switch that shuts the unit down if the pan fills.
  • Look at attic and closet units at the start of each cooling season.
