A leaking appliance is an emergency once water reaches the floor. We answer 24/7, nights and weekends included, with same-day response when available. While you wait, close the supply valve and unplug the machine only if the outlet is dry.

The appliances that cause the most water losses.

  • Washing machines, where supply hoses and drain hoses can split or pop off.
  • Dishwashers, where door gaskets and inlet valves fail.
  • Refrigerators with ice makers, where a small supply line leaks behind the unit.
  • Water softeners and humidifiers, which hold water and use small fittings.

Renting or managing a house near the campus? See water damage near Stony Brook University for who to call first.

The first sign is often a damp floor, a musty smell or a stain on the ceiling below the appliance. Slow leaks can run for weeks behind a machine before anyone notices.

Appliance leak emergency: stop the water first.

First steps.

  • Turn off the appliance and unplug it only if the cord and outlet are dry..
  • Close the supply valve behind or beside the appliance. If you cannot find it, close the main water shutoff..
  • Move small items out of the water and photograph the damage..
  • Do not push a washer or dishwasher across a wet floor, because the weight can damage flooring..
  • Never reach behind a wet appliance if the outlet is damp. If you smell gas from a range or dryer, leave and call 911 or your utility first..

What we do after an appliance leak.

We remove standing water, check the floor, cabinet toe-kicks and walls for moisture and decide what has to be removed and what can be dried in place. Cabinets and subfloors beneath appliances need attention because they trap water.

Why a quick drying plan matters.

Laminate, vinyl plank and particleboard cabinets react quickly to water. Hardwood and tile need careful drying. The longer wet materials sit, the more likely they are to swell, warp or support mold growth.

When water has soaked a floor deeply, we use structural drying equipment to pull moisture from the floor system rather than waiting for it to evaporate.

Prevention that actually helps.

  • Replace rubber washer hoses with braided stainless steel hoses on a regular schedule.
  • Check hoses and connections for bulges, rust and drips a couple of times a year.
  • Leave a gap between the appliance and the wall so hoses do not kink.
  • Know where the shutoff valves are for every appliance.
