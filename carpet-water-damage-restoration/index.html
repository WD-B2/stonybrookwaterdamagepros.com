Carpet and pad: two materials, two outcomes.

Carpet fibers dry fairly quickly when they get proper airflow. The cushion under the carpet is a different story. Foam and rebond pad hold water, and it takes a long time to dry beneath a carpet that is stretched tight.

In a clean water loss, we often lift the carpet, remove the pad and dry the carpet and subfloor separately. In a contaminated water loss, both usually go.

What you can do before the crew arrives.

Helpful first steps.

  • Remove small furniture and items from the wet carpet..
  • Place foil or plastic under the legs of heavy furniture that must stay..
  • Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet..
  • Do not walk on wet carpet more than needed, because it spreads water and dirt..

Why smell and mold matter.

A damp carpet pad can develop an odor within days. If the pad stays wet, mold can grow on it and the subfloor beneath. See mold remediation for how mold is handled if it appears.

Common sources of wet carpet.

  • Basement flooding or seepage. See basement water damage.
  • Water heater, washer and appliance leaks.
  • Overflowing tubs, toilets and sinks.
  • Storm water and roof leaks.

What we check under the carpet.

The subfloor decides whether the job ends with a dry room or a recurring odor. Plywood and OSB soak up water and can swell at the seams. Concrete holds moisture and releases it slowly. We read both with a meter and dry them before any new pad goes down.

We also look at the tack strips, the baseboards and the bottom of the wall. Tack strips rust and stain when wet, and baseboards wick water from the floor line. These are small items that cause big callbacks when they are missed.

If the carpet is kept, we clean it after extraction and set airflow across it until it is dry to the touch and to the meter. Ask us about the pad and what replacement involves before work begins.
