Signs that water may be leaking under your slab.

Warm spots on the floor.

A hot water line leaking under the slab can warm one patch of flooring.

Water bill jumps.

A spike with no change in use points to water going somewhere you cannot see.

Damp or lifting flooring.

Laminate, hardwood and tile can cup, buckle or loosen as moisture pushes up.

Sound of running water.

A faint hiss or trickle when every fixture is off is worth investigating.

Any one sign can have another cause. Two or more together make a leak worth confirming quickly. A plumber can locate and pressure test the line while we handle the damage and the drying.

What to do while you wait for a plumber.

  • Close the main water valve to slow the leak..
  • Keep electronics and rugs off damp flooring..
  • Photograph any warm spots, stains or lifting boards..
  • Do not drill or cut into the slab yourself..
  • If a warm patch is near wiring or you smell gas, leave the area and call 911 or your utility before calling us..

What slab leaks do to a house.

Water under a slab finds its way up through cracks, joints and the edges where the slab meets the wall. It wets the underside of flooring and the bottom of drywall, and it can stay trapped under finished surfaces where air cannot reach.

Because the water arrives from below, damage often hides until a floor starts to move. By then the moisture may have spread across several rooms. That is where moisture mapping matters.

When a slab leak is an emergency.

Water trapped under flooring is a perfect setting for mold. If a slab leak has gone on for weeks, our mold remediation page explains how mold is assessed and handled when it is found.

A slab leak is also a repeat-loss risk. If the pipe is not fixed properly, the floor will wet again. We recommend confirming the repair before we install new finishes.

A word about insurance and slab leaks.

Policies differ on leaks that develop slowly or originate under a foundation. The New York Department of Financial Services advises policyholders to file promptly and keep records. Photograph the damage, keep receipts and ask your insurer what documentation it needs.
