A burst pipe is an emergency that grows by the minute. We answer 24/7, including nights and weekends, with same-day response when available. While you wait, close the main shutoff and open a faucet on the lowest floor to relieve pressure.

A burst pipe is an emergency from the first minute.

Pressurized supply lines keep pushing water until someone closes a valve. A split in a half-inch line can soak a room quickly, and a failure inside a wall or above a ceiling can run for a long time before anyone notices the stain.

Your first job is to stop the flow. Our job starts after that, and it covers everything the water touched, including the places you cannot see.

What to do before help arrives.

  • Find the main shutoff valve and close it. In many houses it sits near the water meter or where the line enters the basement..
  • Open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain pressure from the system once the main is closed..
  • Turn off the breaker to any room with wet outlets or dripping ceiling fixtures, but only if you can reach the panel dry..
  • Take photos and video of the burst section and every wet surface before cleanup begins..
  • If water is dripping through light fixtures or you smell gas, leave the area and call 911 or your utility before you call us..

Where the water goes after a pipe fails.

Inside the wall.

Water runs down the cavity, soaks insulation and drywall, and can reach the floor below without any visible puddle.

Through the ceiling.

Water pools on the drywall above, which sags, stains and can drop without warning.

Under the floor.

Water flows across the subfloor and into the joist bays, where it stays damp long after the surface looks dry.

Down to the basement.

Water follows pipes and framing downward and shows up far from the break itself.

That is why we map moisture with meters and a thermal camera instead of relying on what is visible. Wet insulation and drywall behind an intact paint surface are common finds.

What our crew does on a burst pipe call.

  • Confirm the water is off and the area is safe to enter..
  • Extract standing water with pumps and wand tools, as described on our water extraction page..
  • Open or remove wet materials that cannot be dried in place, such as saturated insulation or sagging drywall..
  • Set air movers and dehumidifiers and monitor them with moisture readings. See structural drying for the method..
  • Record readings and photos so the claim file shows the starting condition and the finish..

The repair question: who fixes the pipe.

We focus on the water damage, not the plumbing. A licensed plumber repairs or replaces the failed pipe, and we coordinate timing so drying equipment is not set up before the leak is truly fixed. If the pipe is still weeping, drying will not hold.

Once the structure is dry, rebuilding the opened wall or ceiling follows. We explain which materials can stay and which have to come out, and we put that scope in front of you before work starts.

Typical causes we see in older plumbing and in winter.

  • Corroded or aging supply lines that finally split at a joint.
  • Frozen sections that cracked during a cold snap. See our page on frozen pipe damage.
  • Failed fittings or connectors under sinks and at appliances.
  • Pressure spikes that stress weak points in the system.

Burst pipes and homeowners insurance in New York.

The New York State Department of Financial Services advises policyholders to file claims promptly, photograph the damage before cleanup and make only temporary repairs until the insurer inspects. Sudden pipe bursts are often treated differently from flood damage, which is generally covered only by a separate flood policy. Read your policy or ask your agent how your plan responds.

Our moisture logs and photos are written to be useful in a claim conversation, and you keep full control of how you proceed with your insurer.
