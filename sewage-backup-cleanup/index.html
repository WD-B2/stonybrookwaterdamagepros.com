A sewage backup is an emergency because the water is contaminated. We take calls 24/7 and offer same-day response when available, including after hours. While you wait, keep everyone out of the area and stop using drains and toilets.

Signs of a sewage or septic backup.

Gurgling drains.

Sounds from toilets and tubs when other fixtures run can mean a blockage.

Sewer odor.

A strong smell near floor drains, basements or bathrooms.

Water coming up.

Toilets, tubs or floor drains that bring dirty water into the house.

Slow drains everywhere.

Several fixtures draining slowly at once suggests a main line problem.

If you see or smell sewage, treat it as contaminated water and avoid contact until it is cleaned.

Sewage backup emergency: what to do first.

Protect yourself and others.

  • Keep children, pets and anyone with health concerns out of the area..
  • Do not flush toilets or run water that drains to the affected line..
  • Avoid touching the water or contaminated items. Wash with soap and clean water if you do..
  • Do not use household fans or vacuums on sewage..
  • Photograph the affected areas from a safe distance..
  • If sewage reaches outlets or you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or your utility before calling us..

A plumber or septic contractor must clear or repair the line. We handle the contaminated water, the materials and the cleaning.

Why sewage is treated differently.

Sewage water is classed as the most contaminated category in the industry standard for water damage. Porous materials that touch it, such as carpet, pad, drywall and insulation, are usually removed rather than cleaned. Hard surfaces can be cleaned and disinfected.

Septic and cesspool realities on Long Island.

Suffolk County reports in its Septic Improvement Program FAQ that 74% of Suffolk County residents rely on antiquated cesspools and septic systems. In Stony Brook, six houses were damaged in the August 2024 storm, including cesspools. Many properties here depend on private systems, and heavy rain or a failing system can send water backward into the building.

If your home uses a septic or cesspool system, a failure outside can show up as sewage inside. A septic contractor evaluates the system. We handle the interior cleanup.

What happens after the cleanup.

When the contaminated materials are out and the surfaces are cleaned, the structure still has to dry. We set drying equipment and monitor it until readings reach the dry standard. Then we describe what has to be rebuilt, such as drywall, baseboards and flooring, so you can plan the repair.

If an outdoor system caused the backup, do not use the affected plumbing until the contractor says it is safe. Using it too early can send more water into the house and undo the cleanup.

Insurance and sewage backups.

Many homeowners policies treat sewer backup as an optional endorsement. The New York Department of Financial Services recommends reading your policy and calling your agent. Document the damage and keep records.
