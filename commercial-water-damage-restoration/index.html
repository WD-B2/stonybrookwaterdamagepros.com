A commercial water loss is an emergency because downtime costs money. Our line is open 24/7, so after-hours discoveries get answered, and same-day response is offered when available. While you wait, keep staff out of wet areas and move inventory off the floor.

Commercial water emergency: why every hour costs more.

A home leak affects a household. A commercial leak affects customers, staff, inventory and neighbors in a shared building. Revenue stops while the space is wet, and landlords, tenants and insurers all need records.

We plan commercial jobs around the building: how many people are affected, where inventory sits, what systems share walls and floors and when the business needs to reopen.

Property types we handle.

What to do before a crew arrives.

  • Stop the source if you can do it safely and keep staff and customers out of wet areas..
  • Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water..
  • Photograph the damage and move inventory, files and equipment off wet floors..
  • Tell the property manager, landlord or insurer in writing..
  • If water reaches panels, sprinkler lines or a gas appliance, evacuate and call 911 or your utility before calling us..

Large losses and insurance.

Larger commercial losses often involve several parties: the owner, the tenant, the property manager and one or more insurers. The New York Department of Financial Services advises policyholders to file claims promptly, photograph damage before cleanup and make only temporary repairs until the insurer inspects. Keep an inventory and a log of what you do.

We keep documentation organized so it can be shared with each party. You decide how to proceed with your insurer.

Tenants, landlords and shared walls.

In a multi-tenant building, the water often starts in one unit and affects another. Who is responsible for what depends on the lease and the building. We do not decide that, but we record where the water began, where it traveled and what each space looks like, so the right parties can discuss it with facts in hand.

Shops and restaurants in the village center have their own page. See water damage in Stony Brook Village for how we handle older buildings and businesses that need to stay open.

We also keep an eye on building systems. Wet ceilings can hold light fixtures, sprinkler lines and ductwork, and wet floors can reach electrical raceways. We flag these early so the right trade can check them.

When a loss affects food, medicine or other regulated goods, the business owner decides what to keep or discard. We provide documentation and do not make those calls for you.
